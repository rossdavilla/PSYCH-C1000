# Emotion Game — content spec

Two decks of six. Partner guesses; actor swipes right for a hit, left for a miss.

## Palette

| Hex | Name | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `#FCEADE` | Powder Petal | Page background |
| `#FFFFFF` | White | Cards, step tiles |
| `#25CED1` | Strong Cyan | **Correct** — right swipe, checkmark; universal bar; primary button |
| `#FF8A5B` | Coral Glow | Timer bar; non-universal bar; eyebrow text |
| `#EA526F` | Bubblegum Pink | **Incorrect** — left swipe, X; timer under 5 seconds |
| `#36262C` | Deep Plum *(added)* | Body text and headings |

> The supplied palette has no dark value, so a deep warm plum was added for text — nothing in the five is dark enough to read as body copy on a light background.

## Deck 1 — Universal emotions

Ekman's basic set. Pan-cultural facial expressions with high recognition rates. These are **face-driven** — the expression lives in the brows, eyes and mouth.

| Emotion | Cue to perform |
|---|---|
| Happiness | Duchenne smile — cheeks raised, crow's feet at the eyes |
| Sadness | Inner brows up and together, lip corners down, gaze dropped |
| Anger | Brows down and together, lids tense, lips pressed thin or squared |
| Fear | Brows raised and together, upper lids high, lips stretched back |
| Surprise | Brows high and arched, eyes wide, jaw dropped |
| Disgust | Nose wrinkled, upper lip raised, head pulling back |

> Expect high scores here. Fear/surprise is the one reliable confusion.

## Deck 2 — Non-universal emotions

Lower cross-cultural recognition. These lean on **posture, timing and context** more than on the face — and several have no agreed-upon signal at all, which is why they score worse.

| Emotion | Cue to perform |
|---|---|
| Shame | Shoulders slumped, head down, gaze averted, whole body shrinking inward |
| Amusement | Open-mouth laugh, head tipped back, eyes crinkled, shoulders shaking |
| Smugness | Closed-lip smile pulled to one side, lids lowered, chin up, slow blink |
| Boredom | Slack face, heavy lids, audible sigh, head propped on a hand |
| Confusion | One brow up, squint, head tilted, mouth pulled to one side |
| Concern | Brows drawn up and together, head tilted, leaning in, soft worried gaze |

> Expect lower scores. That gap between decks is the whole point of the activity.

## The teaching payoff

Ekman's famous 90%+ recognition rates come from **forced-choice** studies — participants picked from a list. When people free-label with no word list, recognition drops sharply (Gendron et al.). This game is free-label, so even the universal deck will land below the textbook number, and the non-universal deck will land well below that.

The results screen shows **both deck scores side by side as a bar graph**, plus the **average time to guess** for each deck — where a miss costs the full 15 seconds. Accuracy and speed usually both favor the universal deck, and speed often shows the effect even when accuracy does not. The debrief is yours to lead out loud; the screen just supplies the numbers.

## Design notes

- Instructions must say **face and shoulders** — shame, boredom and smugness are postural, and students default to face-only.
- Don't show the guesser any image of the emotion. The card the actor holds is a word, nothing more.
- Card color must never vary by deck. The decks are shuffled and hidden during play, so a color difference would leak the category before the reveal. All cards are white.
- The results screen carries **no prose** — two bars, two scores, two average times.
